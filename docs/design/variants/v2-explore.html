APW-115 · second round

Widget, tightened.

You picked the iOS-widget direction, so the rest is inside it. Three refinements that differ in exactly three decisions: how colour is used, how the card earns an edge on an arbitrary Windows desktop, and how much air the corners take. Same 336 × 206 frame, same four states, same words.

All renders at 100 % unless marked. Inter stands in for SF Pro; the blue is a stand-in for the Windows system accent. Nothing is signed off — POPUP.md stays authoritative until you pick.
A

Card the one you picked

Exactly the variation you chose. Level colour on the rails, no border, 22 px corners, the card defined by its shadow alone.

  • EdgeA shadow and nothing else. On a light desktop the white card separates cleanly; over a white window it goes soft.
  • ColourRail colour tracks the level: green in normal range, red at 10 %. Instantly readable, and the loudest of the three on a busy desktop.
  • Density16 / 18 padding with 32 px numerals. Roomy at 100 %, and the geometry that gains the most from a 200 % check.
B

Quiet my recommendation

The same layout, calmed down. Rails are monochrome and only turn red for an exception, a 1 px hairline gives the card an edge over any desktop, and the corners come in to 18 px.

  • EdgeOne hairline at 8 % black, 10 % white in dark. The card holds its shape over a white document, not just over wallpaper.
  • ColourMonochrome by default. Colour means “something needs you” — low battery, nothing else. This is closer to Apple’s own macOS accessory popover than A is.
  • Density14 / 16 padding, 30 px numerals, 5 px rails. Slightly tighter than A, and the geometry I want to see at 200 % before committing.
C

Tint the stylised one

An accent-tinted surface, the way iOS 18 tints home-screen widgets. Monochrome accent rails, primary numerals, and the same exception-only warning.

  • EdgeThe tint itself defines the card. It reads as deliberate on any wallpaper, and as foreign the moment the accent is a Windows blue rather than Apple’s.
  • ColourRails take the accent, not the level. Calmer than A, more decorative than B; the low state is the only place red appears.
  • DensityIdentical to A, so the tint is the only variable in the comparison.

Coverage in the recommended variant

B, holding the states the contract calls ugly. The layout never reflows between them: a column only loses its number, never its place.

200 % scale

B at 150 % of this sheet (which is what 100 % looks like at a 150 % Windows scale). Nothing compresses, nothing turns into a dashboard, and the 18 px corners still read as corners.

B is the one I would build

A is the version you liked, and B keeps its whole shape while making two changes that matter on a Windows desktop. The hairline means the card survives being drawn over a white document, which A does not. Neutral rails stop the popup shouting level information nobody asked for; colour then appears only when a component needs attention, which is also how Apple treats the accessory popover on macOS. C is the most decorative and the most fragile: it inherits whatever accent the user has, and an amber or magenta system accent turns it into a different product.

Same content rules as POPUP.md: values in 10 % steps, unknown is an em dash, disconnect follows the endpoint signal, no Apple artwork. If B is chosen, the token table, the corner radius, the rail height and the colour clause all change, and the motion spec carries over untouched.
